# Initiative: Researcher-to-Project Matchmaking 💛

## Scope

Ensuring quality recruitment that is tailored to both the applicant and the project is critical to set up a team for success. By analyzing the current admissions and team-matching process, and analyzing the applications and performance of researchers this past semester, gaps and trends can be observed in the admissions-to-researcher and project performance pipeline. With these observations, this initiative is designed to help systematize how HAAG identifies high-potential research candidates from the existing application responses and match the researchers to projects that have specific needs.

## Current State

I reached out to several HAAG admins and attended an admin meeting to understand the admissions process. Currently, the process of admitting students happens in multiple phases. First, HAAG admins identify top research candidates by assigning them a score based on well-aligned experience and quality responses. Then, based on the retention survey, the project capacity needs are identified. Finally, candidates are selected for projects based on their scores and project capacity requirements. This is an intuitive process by which to place prospective researchers into respective groups.

## Problem Statement

### Researcher Match

The current state, however, also suffers several challenges. From the researcher side, the scoring system by which candidates are scored is somewhat arbitrary—different admins are responsible for reviewing applications across projects, which inherently leads to variation in what would be considered a “good” candidate. While there are general guidelines based on certain factors that are provided to indicate an applicant that would have good potential, HAAG now has access to a wealth of information from the researcher logs and project manager reports that can potentially shed light on trends from indicators that are seen in past accepted applications and how they have translated into the work the researchers have conducted this past semester. For this procedure, I looked at the weekly logs and corresponding researcher applications at a high-level to help identify any broad indicators of how the application may have indicated whether a researcher ended up being highly active, less active (with or without reports), inactive, or dropped the course. These are factored into the procedure that will be shown below to an extent, but there are opportunities for future deep analysis on these inputs and outputs and how HAAG can enhance the both the admissions process and potentially the application itself to yield stronger signals in the future.

### Project Match

The other angle by which one must consider matchmaking is from the project perspective. This upcoming application review cycle will be done with applicants by project, which is a good step in helping to tailor applicant admissions based on the projects. However, “project need” is also still ambiguous—how much does the application process take into account factors beyond capacity consistently, since there are many admins also involved in this process? In the past, these data points were not consolidated into one source of truth to look back on, but this semester, Quinn Mattison is administering a survey to the project managers regarding their thoughts on the project and identifying its needs, which should significantly help to boost project need identification. But there is still a need to standardize how this information is utilized.

## Proposal

Given these challenges, standardizing both ends of the matchmaking process will help to ensure consistency in the HAAG matchmaking process. By standardizing the scoring process from the applicant side and the project needs side, we can help set a consistent standard for admins involved in admissions and matchmaking and help propel HAAG projects forward.

## Procedure

Below, a procedure is provided to walk admins through how to score researchers in each individual category based on their review of the application. An attached Excel template is provided with a structured scoring guide.

**Researcher-to-Project Matchmaking Template**

## Matchmaking Guide

In this guide, we will walk through how to fill out the Project Info sheet and the Researcher Info sheet, which will guide how to determine researcher-project suitability.

## Project Info

On Sheet 2, we have the project info, which needs to be filled out only once per project, unless you want to change the inputs after accepting someone that suitably addresses a high need very well and want complementary candidates. These scores should be primarily based on the survey administered by Quinn that surveyed the managers on the project status/health.

The following information should be filled out:

* **Project Health:** 1 (red), 2 (yellow), or 3 (green)
  This is an indicator of how well the project is doing—whether it is on-track and has sufficient resources to set it up for success. Use answer from project manager survey.

* **Project Completion Percentage:** 0-100
  This can be averaged by the researcher retention and project manager survey results (Q3 for manager survey).

* **Goal to publish in the next term:** 0 (no), 1 (yes)
  Infer from project manager survey (Q2, Q4)

* **More technical skills needed:** 0 (no), 1 (yes)
  Review risk sections of manager survey if this is mentioned (Q4, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q11)

* **More domain knowledge needed:** 0 (no), 1 (yes)
  Review risk sections of manager survey if this is mentioned (Q4, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q11)

* **More research experience needed:** 0 (no), 1 (yes)
  Review risk sections of manager survey if this is mentioned (Q4, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q11)

* **Applications Received:** How many applications were received for the project

* **Capacity Need:** How many researchers are needed beyond returning researchers

These are indicators of how well the project is doing and where the project may need more support, including what skills need to be weighted more heavily when reviewing the researcher side.

## Researcher Info

On Sheet 3, we have the researcher info, where each row will represent 1 researcher. The rubric that will be followed is outlined below, with recommendations on how to score:

### Personal Projects

Review GitHub, personal sites, project links to assess how experimental/the types of projects the researcher enjoys doing in spare time – engagement indicator, especially if in alignment with project topic. Can incorporate this more heavily in final recommendation if project needs more experimentation.

0-5: Low score for no personal projects, middle score for some projects that are not related to the research project, high schore for projects that are relevant to research project

### Course Alignment

Review Q1

0-5: Low scores for no relevant course experience, middle for some experience, high score for high alignment with project experience.

### Technical Background/Skills

Review LinkedIn, GitHub, personal site, projects link, Q1, Q2, Q3. Weigh more heavily if project has extra need for highly-specific technical skills.

### Project Domain Knowledge

Review LinkedIn, GitHub, personal site, projects link, Employer site, research link, Q2, Q3. Weigh more heavily if more project-specific domain knowledge is needed.

0-5: Low score for no domain experience, middle score for personal experience in domain, high score for work/research experience in field.

### Research Background

Review Q3

0-5: Low score for no experience, low-middle score for limited research, middle-higher scores for general research experience, higher scores for relevant research experience, and highest scores for relevant research experience with publication/presentation. Weight more heavily if there are research/publication needs and near future goals.

### Application Proposal Effort

Review Q5

0-5: Low score for low effort/vague/general/regurgitated from the project description answers, middle score for some specificity, high score for high specificity with creative resources or tools leveraged

### Working Style

Review Q6 and Q7

0-5: Low scores for vague or irrelevant tools consulted or default reaching out to peers without putting in effort to research before peer/faculty outreach, high scores for creative resources and research-first approaches

### Recommend for project?

Remember that not all scores should be weighed equally! Review sheet 2 to refresh on project needs and whether the candidate researcher fulfills the needs for the project. Application Proposal Effort should be weighted heavily regardless of project.

0-5: Low scores for having limited HAAG-potential at this time, middle scores for research potential but less relevant experience or less alignment with the project needs, high scores for good alignment with project experience and needs

### Comments/Justification

Fill out for why or why not a candidate is suitable or unsuitable for the project at this time

## Future Work

In the future, a weighting scheme will be implemented, where a final weighted score is generated for how suitable the researcher is to the given project based on the structured inputs. Additionally, since the survey for the project statuses by the project managers was recently administered at the time of this write-up, further refinement would need to be done on what factors are shown to be important based on the survey results, and how they impact the green, yellow, and red project health statuses, so that they can be added into the weighting scheme and make it more sophisticated. Furthermore, as mentioned before, a more robust qualitative analysis to compare application and ultimate researcher activity and output would be important to loop back into the application process. Since recruitment is outside of the scope of the semester, trialing this scheme during a recruitment cycle and evaluating it based on engagement/retention/project progress results and comparing it to historical semesters will be important moving forward.
