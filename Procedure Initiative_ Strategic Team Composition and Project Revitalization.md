# Procedure Initiative: Strategic Team Composition and Project Revitalization

- **Procedure Topic:** Recruitment Initiative
- **Procedure Initiative Manager:** Kaiyuan Deng (kdeng75@gatech.edu)

## 1. Problem Statement

While HAAG successfully recruits over 100 researchers across approximately 30 projects each semester, the team composition process often lacks a systematic approach for rescuing "at-risk" or stalled projects. Mature projects frequently lose momentum when key senior members graduate or leave, leaving the remaining team without strong technical leadership. Currently, new applicants are largely assigned based on their project preferences or general availability. Without a targeted "revitalization" strategy that intentionally identifies struggling projects and strategically injects them with highly capable new recruits, these projects risk stagnation or failure.

## 2. Initiative Goal

The goal of this initiative is to design, evaluate, and standardize a data-driven "Revitalization Matching Procedure" for the HAAG Admin team. This procedure will provide a reproducible recipe for identifying at-risk projects at the end of a semester and strategically assigning top-tier incoming applicants (based on FAIR-CS evaluation dimensions) to re-energize those specific teams.

## 3. Deliverables

- A standardized "Project Health & Revitalization Rubric" for identifying at-risk teams.
- A step-by-step "Recipe" (Standard Operating Procedure) for the Admin team to execute the strategic matching process during the Week 0 recruitment phase.
- An auditing framework to ensure this procedure remains sustainable across future semesters.

## 4. Initiative Framework

### 4.1. Discovery Phase

To understand the current baseline and historical bottlenecks in team composition, I will:
- Analyze the "Master HAAG Enrollment Excel" from the previous and current semesters to identify projects that suffered high turnover or stalled progress (e.g., zero returning members, or projects that failed to produce a first draft publication).
- Conduct brief interviews with HAAG Admin members (like John Hagood or Charlie) who managed the sorting process this semester, asking specific questions about how they currently decide which student goes to which project when there are overlapping preferences.
- Review the FAIR-CS paper's guidelines on applicant evaluation (Planning detail, Technical ability, Technical knowledge, and Willingness to collaborate) to understand the existing metrics we already collect from applicants.

### 4.2. Hypothesis

If HAAG Admins utilize a standardized "Project Health Rubric" to flag at-risk projects prior to the application review phase, and deliberately prioritize the placement of top-scoring applicants (specifically those scoring highest in 'Technical ability' and 'Planning detail') into these flagged projects, then early-semester momentum will increase, and the failure rate of mature projects will decrease.

### 4.3. Experiment & The Procedure (The Recipe)

Since the current semester's recruitment has concluded, I will validate this framework through a cognitive walkthrough and historical data mapping, simulating how this recipe would be applied to the upcoming semester.

**Step-by-Step Procedure for Future Admins:**

1.  **Project Health Audit (Week -2 before semester starts):** The Recruitment Lead opens the previous semester's Operations Tracker and Master Enrollment Doc. The Lead tags a project as "Code Red / Needs Revitalization" if it meets two or more of the following criteria:
    - The project lost its Computational Advisor.
    - The project has 0-1 returning researchers.
    - The previous project manager reported "Cold" status for more than 3 consecutive weeks.

2.  **Candidate Identification (Week -1 during application screening):** During the standard application review, the Admin team scores applicants on the 10-point scale across the four FAIR-CS dimensions. The Recruitment Lead explicitly filters the applicant pool to identify "Revitalizer Candidates" — applicants who score an 8 or higher in both "Technical Ability" and "Planning Detail", indicating they can work independently and lead technically.

3.  **Strategic Assignment & Handoff (Week 0):** Before fulfilling general project preferences, the Recruitment Lead manually assigns at least one "Revitalizer Candidate" to each "Code Red" project. The Admin sends a customized onboarding message to the Revitalizer Candidate, transparently framing their assignment as a leadership opportunity: "You have been specifically selected for this project due to your strong technical background to help drive this established project toward its publication goals."

## 5. Metrics for Evaluation

To evaluate the success of this procedure once implemented, HAAG will track:

- **Time-to-First-Contribution (TTFC):** Measuring if the revitalized project achieves its first meaningful commit/artifact faster than it did in the previous semester.
- **Project Retention Rate:** Tracking the drop-out rate of researchers in "Code Red" projects compared to the historical baseline.
- **Manager Temperature Checks:** Monitoring the weekly operations tracker to see if the assigned Project Manager transitions the team from "Cold" to "Neutral" or "Hot" within the first 4 weeks.

## 6. Report & Continuous Auditing

Following the Week 7 principle of system sustainability, this procedure includes a continuous auditing step. At the end of the recruitment cycle, the Recruitment Lead will review the "Code Red" assignments and update the flagging criteria based on feedback from the Computational Advisors. The finalized procedure will be hosted in the Recruitment GitHub repo as a living document for the next Admin successor.

## 7. Intended Audience

- **Primary:** Future HAAG Recruitment Leads and the Admin Team who need a reproducible manual to execute team assignments without relying on tribal knowledge.
- **Secondary:** Project Managers assigned to "Code Red" teams, providing them context on why their team was constructed this way.

## 8. Organizational Need Being Served

This initiative serves HAAG's critical need for Organizational Stability and Project Health. By moving away from random or purely preference-based assignments and treating team composition as a strategic resource allocation, HAAG can prevent valuable research projects from dying out simply due to natural student turnover.
