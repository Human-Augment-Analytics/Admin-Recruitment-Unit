### 2. How are you assessing your role as the project manager and its effect on the overall project?

I am assessing my role as the project manager by looking at whether my work improves clarity, coordination, and continuity for the Vector DB Project. Since this initiative is focused on recruitment and onboarding, my impact is not only measured by technical output but also by how effectively the process helps people join and contribute to the project.

My role is to organize scattered information into a clear procedure, identify gaps in the current process, and create documentation that project leads and contributors can actually use. I am also responsible for making sure the procedure is practical, not overly complicated, and aligned with the needs of the project.

The effect of this role on the overall project should be visible in several ways:

- Project leads should have a clearer way to evaluate candidates.
- New contributors should have fewer unanswered questions during onboarding.
- The project should experience fewer delays caused by missing access, unclear setup steps, or unclear expectations.
- Future semesters should be able to reuse and improve the procedure instead of starting from scratch.

At this stage, I am measuring my effectiveness by progress on documentation, quality of stakeholder feedback, and whether the procedure addresses real onboarding problems rather than theoretical ones.

### 3. What challenges and successes have you observed in your group?

One challenge I have observed is that recruitment and onboarding knowledge can be distributed across multiple people, tools, and prior semester experiences. This makes it difficult for new contributors to know where to begin and makes it harder for project leads to maintain consistency.

Another challenge is that project needs may change each semester. A recruitment procedure must be general enough to reuse but specific enough to help the Vector DB Project select contributors with the right skills, availability, and motivation.

I have also observed that onboarding is not only a documentation problem. Even if documents exist, new contributors may still need clear starter tasks, mentorship, and communication expectations. This means the procedure must include both information and action steps.

The main success so far is that the initiative has a clear structure. The recruitment process has been broken down into manageable parts: announcement, application collection, shortlisting, evaluation, selection, onboarding, and first-week milestones. This structure makes the work easier to document and easier for stakeholders to review.

Another success is that the initiative directly addresses sustainability. By planning to host the procedure in GitHub and potentially use Slack pinned posts or channel modules, the process can become part of the project’s normal operating model.

### 4. How are you collaborating with stakeholders to ensure your role is supporting and motivating the group?

I am collaborating with stakeholders by focusing on their needs and reducing the amount of extra work required from them. For project leadership, the goal is to provide a procedure that makes recruitment and onboarding easier, not more bureaucratic. For contributors, the goal is to create documentation that answers practical questions and helps them become productive faster.

My collaboration approach includes:

- Asking project leads what they need from new contributors.
- Asking experienced contributors what onboarding information was missing or confusing.
- Reviewing historical communication to identify repeated questions.
- Drafting documentation in a format that stakeholders can easily review.
- Keeping the process lightweight and reusable.
- Designing templates that future project leads can adapt.

This approach supports and motivates the group because it reduces confusion and gives everyone a clearer understanding of expectations. It also helps contributors feel that their time is respected because they are not forced to search through scattered information.
